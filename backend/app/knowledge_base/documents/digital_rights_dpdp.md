### Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023
The DPDP Act governs the processing of digital personal data in India.
1. **Right to Erasure/Data Deletion (Section 12)**: A Data Principal (citizen) has the right to demand the erasure of their personal data from a Data Fiduciary (corporation/app) if the data is no longer necessary for the purpose it was collected, or if consent is withdrawn.
2. **Right to Withdraw Consent (Section 6)**: Citizens can withdraw their consent at any time. The Data Fiduciary must cease processing the data and ensure their Data Processors also cease processing.
3. **Data Breach Notification (Section 8)**: In the event of a personal data breach (e.g., un-consented leaks), the Data Fiduciary must intimate the Data Protection Board and the affected Data Principal.
4. **Complaint to Data Protection Board**: Citizens can file complaints with the Data Protection Board for violations. The Board can impose significant penalties (up to ₹250 crore) for severe breaches.

### Information Technology (IT) Act, 2000
1. **Section 43A**: Where a body corporate possesses sensitive personal data and is negligent in implementing reasonable security practices, causing wrongful loss, they are liable to pay compensation.
2. **Section 72A**: Punishment for disclosure of information in breach of lawful contract (e.g., unauthorized data brokers selling data). Penalty includes imprisonment up to 3 years or fine up to ₹5 lakh.
