### Public Nuisance under Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) / IPC
1. **Definition**: Under Section 268 of IPC (and corresponding Section 278 of BNS), a person is guilty of a public nuisance if they do any act or illegal omission which causes any common injury, danger, or annoyance to the public or to people who occupy property in the vicinity.
2. **Actionable Nuisances**: Unmanaged open trash burning, loud night-time speakers (violating noise pollution rules), and illegal construction dust fall under this category.
3. **Remedy (Section 133 CrPC / 152 BNSS)**: A citizen can file a petition before a District Magistrate (DM) or Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM) seeking a conditional order to remove the nuisance. The DM has immediate powers to order the stoppage of the nuisance (e.g., halting illegal construction, stopping loudspeakers).

### Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981 & Water Act, 1974
1. **Emissions & Dust**: Under the Air Act, no person operating any industrial plant can emit air pollutants in excess of the standards laid down by the State Pollution Control Board (SPCB). Illegal construction dust and industrial smoke violate this.
2. **Chemical Dumping**: Under Section 24 of the Water Act, knowingly causing or permitting any poisonous, noxious, or polluting matter to enter into any stream, well, or sewer is strictly prohibited.
3. **Grievance**: Citizens can file direct complaints with the respective State Pollution Control Board (e.g., DPCC in Delhi, MPCB in Maharashtra) or the National Green Tribunal (NGT) for severe violations.
