### Right to Public Services (RTS) Legislation
Right to Service Acts guarantee time-bound delivery of public services (birth certificates, licenses, property registrations) and mandate penalties for defaulting officers.

### State-Specific Guarantees
1. **Karnataka (Sakala Services Act, 2011)**: Guarantees services within stipulated time limits. If delayed, the citizen is entitled to a compensatory allowance of ₹20 per day (up to a maximum of ₹500) payable by the defaulting designated officer.
2. **Maharashtra (Right to Public Services Act, 2015)**: Ensures transparent and time-bound delivery. If an official fails to provide the service without a valid reason, they can be penalized between ₹500 to ₹5,000, and a portion may be awarded to the applicant.
3. **Delhi (Right to Citizen's Time Bound Delivery of Services Act, 2011)**: Officers failing to provide services within the deadline are liable for a penalty of ₹10 per day, up to a maximum of ₹200. The penalty is deducted directly from the officer's salary and paid to the citizen as compensation.
4. **Uttar Pradesh (Janhit Guarantee Act, 2011)**: Imposes penalties ranging from ₹500 to ₹5,000 on designated officers for unjustified delays. The appellate authority can order this penalty.
5. **Rajasthan (Guaranteed Delivery of Public Services Act, 2011)**: Officers face a penalty of ₹250 per day of delay (up to ₹5,000) for failing to deliver services within the notified time. 
6. **Tamil Nadu**: While Tamil Nadu relies heavily on Citizen's Charters and e-Sevai portals for service delivery rather than a standalone penal RTS Act, citizens can escalate deliberate administrative delays to the Directorate of Vigilance and Anti-Corruption (DVAC) or file grievances on the CM Helpline.

### Grievance Escalation
If a service is delayed, the citizen must file a First Appeal to the designated Appellate Authority under the respective state's Act, demanding the service delivery and the statutory delay penalty (compensation) from the defaulting officer.
