### Filing Municipal Complaints
Citizens can file complaints regarding civic services through multiple channels: (1) Ward Office in person at the Citizen Facilitation Centre; (2) Municipal Corporation helpline (1916 for BMC/Mumbai); (3) Online portals such as MyBMC for Mumbai, Aaple Sarkar for Maharashtra state; (4) Mobile apps (Swachhata-MoHUA app for sanitation, MyBMC app). Upon filing, a unique Docket/Complaint ID is issued via SMS/email with the expected resolution date (SLA).

### Service Level Agreements (SLAs)
Municipal corporations maintain standard timelines: Garbage/Sanitation complaints: 12-24 hours. Water supply issues (burst pipe, no supply): 24-72 hours. Pothole/road repair: 48-72 hours. Streetlight repair: 24-48 hours. Drainage/sewer blockage: 24-48 hours. Encroachment removal: 15-30 days. If the complaint is not resolved within SLA, it automatically escalates to the next level officer.

### Escalation Hierarchy
Level 1: Junior Engineer / Sanitary Inspector (field level). Level 2: Assistant Engineer / Executive Engineer (technical supervision). Level 3: Assistant Municipal Commissioner / Ward Officer (ward administration). Level 4: Deputy Municipal Commissioner / Additional Commissioner (zonal head). Level 5: Municipal Commissioner / Mayor (apex executive). Citizens should escalate in order and maintain written records of each escalation.

### Using RTI for Civic Issues
When municipal complaints go unresolved, citizens can file RTI applications to obtain: (1) Action Taken Report (ATR) on the specific complaint; (2) Budget allocated vs. actually spent on the specific service (road repair, garbage collection) in the ward; (3) Names and designations of officers responsible for the service in the area; (4) Contractor details — name, contract value, terms, performance metrics — for outsourced civic works; (5) Inspection reports and site visit records; (6) Scheduled timetable for garbage collection, water supply hours, sweeping schedules. Filing an RTI creates accountability pressure and often accelerates resolution of pending complaints.

### Lokshahi Din (Public Hearing Day)
In Maharashtra, citizens can present unresolved grievances on Lokshahi Din: held on the 1st Monday of every month at Ward level, 2nd Monday at District Collector level. This is a formal grievance hearing where officers must respond to complaints on record. Citizens should bring all documentation: original complaint receipt, complaint ID, follow-up correspondence, and any RTI replies received.

### Legal Remedies Beyond Municipal Process
If municipal processes fail: (1) File RTI under Section 6(1) demanding certified copies of ATR, inspection reports, and officer accountability; (2) Approach State Lokayukta for complaints of corruption or willful neglect; (3) File complaint under Consumer Protection Act, 2019 for deficiency in service if specific fees/taxes were paid; (4) In extreme cases of statutory duty failure endangering public safety, file Writ of Mandamus under Article 226 of the Constitution before the High Court.
