### Section 7 — Standard Rent
The standard rent of any premises shall be the rent at which the premises were let on the date of commencement of this Act, or where the premises were not let on such date, the rent at which they were last let before such date. Where no standard rent is fixed, the Court may fix the standard rent at such amount as, having regard to the provisions of this Act and the circumstances of the case, the Court deems just.

### Sections 15 & 16 — Protection Against Eviction and Grounds for Eviction
Section 15(1): A tenant shall not be evicted from premises except in accordance with the provisions of this Act. No suit for recovery of possession shall be instituted by a landlord against a tenant for possession of any premises so long as the tenant pays rent and performs the conditions of the tenancy.
Section 15(2): No suit for eviction on grounds of non-payment of rent shall be instituted unless the landlord has given the tenant a written notice of demand and the tenant has failed to make payment within 90 days.
Section 16(1) Grounds: (a) Tenant has committed acts causing substantial and permanent damage; (b) erected permanent structures without written permission; (c) conduct causing nuisance or annoyance to adjoining occupiers; (e)(ii) unlawful subletting, assignment or transfer; (g) premises reasonably and bona fide required by landlord for own occupation; (i) premises required for repairs/demolition/reconstruction that cannot be carried out without tenant vacating; (n) premises not used without reasonable cause for 6 continuous months for the purpose for which let.

### Security Deposit Rules
Under modern residential Leave and License agreements in Maharashtra, security deposits are typically limited to 2-3 months' rent for residential premises. The deposit must be returned within a reasonable period (typically within 1 month) of the licensee vacating the premises and handing over peaceful possession, minus legitimate deductions for: unpaid rent/license fee, unpaid utility bills, damage to the premises beyond normal wear and tear. The landlord/licensor cannot unreasonably withhold the deposit. Under Section 18, receiving any sum as premium (pugree) in addition to standard rent is illegal and punishable.

### Section 23 — Essential Services and Section 29 — Cut-off of Essential Supply
Section 23: Every tenant shall be entitled to receive essential services including water supply, electricity, sanitation facilities, and lift service. The landlord is bound to provide and maintain these services.
Section 29: If the landlord or any person acting on behalf of the landlord cuts off or withholds any essential supply or service enjoyed by the tenant, the Court shall, on the application of the tenant, make an order directing the landlord to restore such supply or service. Violation is punishable with imprisonment up to 3 months or fine up to Rs. 1,000. The landlord cannot disconnect water, electricity, or other essential supplies as a means of pressuring the tenant to vacate.

### Section 14 — Repairs
Section 14(1): The landlord shall keep the premises in good and tenantable repair and shall be bound to make all necessary repairs.
Section 14(2): If the landlord neglects to make any repairs which he is bound to make within 30 days of receiving written notice from the tenant, the tenant may cause such repairs to be made and deduct the expense from rent payable. Such deduction shall not exceed one-fourth (25%) of the annual rent in any one year.

### Section 24 & 55 — Leave and License Agreements
Section 55: Every agreement for Leave and License or for tenancy entered into after commencement of this Act must be in writing and compulsorily registered under the Registration Act, 1908. The responsibility of registration lies with the landlord/licensor. Failure to register is punishable with imprisonment up to 3 months and/or fine up to Rs. 5,000. In case of dispute about terms, the tenant's contention shall prevail unless rebutted by a registered agreement.
Section 24: A licensee in occupation after expiry of the license period must deliver possession. The licensor may approach the Competent Authority for eviction. If the licensee fails to vacate, damages at double the license fee may be awarded until possession is surrendered.

### Notice Periods
For monthly tenancies: minimum 1 month notice period. For tenancies of more than one year: notice period as specified in agreement, minimum 3 months. For Leave and License agreements: notice as per agreement terms, typically 1-3 months. A landlord cannot evict without giving proper notice and obtaining court order. Even after lease expiry, a tenant in rent-controlled premises cannot be evicted without a court order on valid grounds under Section 16.
